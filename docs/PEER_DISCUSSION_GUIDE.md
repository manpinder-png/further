# Further: peer discussion guide

Use the [interactive story](FURTHER_DISCUSSION_SLIDES.html) to explain Further before comparing candidate directions. It accompanies requirements draft v0.3. It does not approve scope or implement the product.

## The story in one sentence

An adult learner expresses an imperfect need, Further understands the current context and finds a willing person, and the same activity continues when the need or interface changes.

## A six-minute route

| Chapter | What to show | Question for peers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The moment | Nora has 15 minutes and wants a person to reason with about GPUs. | Have we experienced this gap between an answer and a useful person? |
| The intent | Reveal the illustrative interpretation of a rough question. | What is the minimum context needed to begin without lengthy onboarding? |
| The fit | Change peer exploration to experienced help, or remove availability. | Which fit conditions must be respected before a suggestion is useful? |
| The change | Apply the correction, switch to the proposed host view, and return. | What must continue, and what should stay a temporary exception? |
| The connection | Accept on one side, then both sides. | What interaction would prove value for both people? |
| The limit | Make sample people unavailable, then choose to wait or review constraints. | Which no-match behavior is honest and useful? |
| The proof | Review genuine evidence and explicit simplifying boundaries. | What can we demonstrate by the deadline? |
| The decision | Compare actual team proposals, then record the outcome and boundary. | Which direction should we pursue, and what do we defer? |

## Explain the intelligence with the change

The key moment is an authorized conversation revealing a need for experienced help with a specific Python training problem. Further considers that signal alongside its continuing understanding of Nora and the live community state.

The current need changes. Her usual preference for learning with peers remains unless she separately confirms a lasting change. Different criteria may still recommend the same person. The need, explanation, willingness, and availability must be current.

The proposed Muse connector should use the same ongoing activity and Further-owned intelligence. The host's identity, access, and operations remain unverified.

## Separate an explanation from a product claim

People and interpretations in the presentation are fictional. Matching uses explicit example constraints. There are no model calls, real presence, shared room, or verified host integration.

These illustrations explain desired behavior. The final hackathon demonstration must independently prove the defining live behavior with actual participants and Meta use. Limited infrastructure and synthetic tests need clear labels. Global-scale capability is unproven.

Live events, watch parties, enrichment, and additional input modes remain broader opportunities. Include them only through an explicit scope tradeoff.

## A fair decision conversation

1. Give each peer the same opportunity to explain their candidate direction, user, problem, and minimum demonstration.
2. Compare every proposal using the same questions: user value, a useful distinction from current assistants, and a credible live proof within the available time. Discuss important dependencies and risks.
3. Agree the voting method first. This worksheet provides a simple manual tally. It does not enforce voter identity, one vote per person, or a particular decision rule.
4. Enter actual choices once on a shared facilitator screen. Add real alternatives. Further starts with zero votes and receives no invented score or preset advantage.
5. Resolve ties openly. Record the rationale, business outcome sentence, first demo boundary, explicit deferrals, and open questions.
6. Download the decision record. A local tally is not an independently verified election and does not automatically approve requirements.
7. Explicitly agree and version the business baseline before deriving technical requirements, stories, tasks, and owners.

Candidate outcome sentence for discussion:

> For adults exploring a technical concept, Further should turn an imperfect current need into a useful, willing human connection, with correctable context that continues across supported interfaces.

This is a statement to review, not a team commitment.

## Original application baseline

The verified application selected **Indie Dev Track** and described **Build for Your World**. It proposed one adult technical community, Meta-assisted clarification, live matching, explicit waiting/no-match handling, short human sessions, prompts and a teach-back check, and measures of wait time, usefulness, independent understanding, and repeat use.

The current requirements expand that social journey with continuing user understanding and contextual connector access. Intentionally decide the role of facilitation and explain-back, rather than treating the application proposal as implemented or silently changing its emphasis.

The personal application record stays separate from this public repository. It does not establish a launched product or proven learning result. Participant eligibility and team submission requirements also apply.

## Practical use

The story works offline as one HTML file. Small screens stack and scroll rather than shrinking text. Print story supplies an eight-chapter print layout.

Choices and notes use local browser storage when available. Devices do not synchronize. If storage is blocked, the presentation explains that limit. Download the record for a portable copy. Do not enter private account information.

The examples explain the business concept. They do not replace the final live demonstration.
